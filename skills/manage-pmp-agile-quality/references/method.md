# Tailored Delivery Method

## Foundation

Use the current PMI model as a decision framework, not as one prescriptive
lifecycle. Assess project needs, complexity, uncertainty, criticality, value,
stakeholders, and business environment before choosing predictive, adaptive, or
hybrid delivery.

Primary sources:

- https://www.pmi.org/standards/pmbok
- https://www.pmi.org/standards/agile
- https://www.pmi.org/learning/agile/disciplined-agile
- https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/certifications/new-pmp-examination-content-outline-2026

## Decision rules

Prefer predictive delivery when requirements and technology are stable and
feedback latency is acceptable.

Prefer adaptive delivery when requirements or technology are highly uncertain,
increments are inexpensive and reversible, and stakeholders can provide
frequent feedback.

Prefer hybrid delivery when governance, compliance, architecture, integration,
or irreversible delivery constraints must stay stable while product discovery
and implementation benefit from iterations.

## Operating model

Maintain three concurrent loops:

1. Value: vision, backlog, increment, review, feedback, reorder.
2. Quality: requirements, metrics, prevention, assurance, control,
   improvement.
3. Governance: evidence, risk/issue/change assessment, decision, plan update.

Do not wait for project closure to learn. Capture observations during work,
evaluate them during review or retrospective, and store only reusable,
evidence-backed lessons as enterprise experience.

## People and communication

Maintain a shared vision, clear roles, transparent progress, stakeholder
feedback, knowledge transfer, and an escalation path. Let the delivery team own
its detailed way of working within governance and quality boundaries.
